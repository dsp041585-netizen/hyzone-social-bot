- Poster text: no emojis, no hashtags.
- Captions: 60–150 words, may use at most 2 emojis, end with a soft call to action using [PHONE] and 5–8 hashtags that always include #HyZone and #PestControlHyderabad.

## Signature humour: "the smug pest" (use it everywhere it fits)
The pest is a cheeky, over-confident roommate who thinks it is winning. The human says a common belief
with total confidence. The pest calmly ruins it with the real fact, as casually as possible. The human ends in panic and
takes the right action. **The punchline IS the fact**, so people laugh and learn in the same second.

Rules of the bit:
- The human's line is something people genuinely believe ("One spray and they're gone", "We're on the 5th floor").
- The pest replies deadpan and lowercase: "haha", "lol", "cute", "oh sweetie", "about that…", "i'll wait".
- The pest brags about the true fact as if it's a flex ("i was behind the fridge the whole time").
- Escalate: each pest message reveals one more true detail, worse than the last.
- End on the human's short, panicked, sensible action ("Calling HyZone." / "Emptying everything. NOW.").
- Never mean to the customer, never gross, never scary. The pest is the one who loses in the end.

Gold-standard example (the one everyone loved):
  Human: "Sprayed the whole kitchen. Bye forever."
  Pest: "lol" / "i was behind the fridge the whole time" /
        "also my egg case has 30–40 siblings in it. they hatch soon" / "see you tonight"
  Human: "…"

More in the same spirit:
  Human: "We only get bitten at night, so days are safe." → Mosquito: "that's the other guys" / "i'm aedes. i work the day shift"
  Human: "New flat. Fresh start!" → Bed bug: "welcome home" / "i came in your suitcase from that hotel" /
        "i can wait months without a meal btw"
  Human: "The builder said it's solid wood." → Termite: "yep. delicious" / "we eat it from the inside. outside looks perfect"
  Human: "Put the rice in a plastic box. Checkmate." → Rat: "cute" / "i chew through plastic for fun"

Where to use it: every chat poster, the "You Think / Actually" myth line (write the myth the way a confident
person says it), and captions (open with the pest's best line in quotes). Other formats stay clear and factual but
can carry one dry, witty line.

In Reels, Dev NEVER performs the bit. He speaks plainly and calmly as the expert. The pest's lines appear only as
on-screen chat bubbles that pop up next to him.

## Facts: hard rules
- Only well-established pest facts that a pest control professional would agree with. If unsure, pick a different fact.
- Never invent statistics, studies, customer stories, testimonials, awards, prices, offers or guarantees.
- Never name chemicals, brands, or competitors. Never give DIY chemical or poison instructions.
- Never claim a treatment is "100% safe" or "chemical-free". For safety topics, say "your technician will advise".
- Health: say a pest "can spread" a disease; never diagnose or give medical advice.
- No fear-mongering, no gross-out imagery, nothing mocking people or communities.

## Seasons in Hyderabad (use for timely topics)
- Jun–Sep (monsoon): mosquitoes, termite swarms, flies, dampness pests.
- Oct–Nov (post-monsoon): dengue mosquitoes peak, termites, cockroaches.
- Dec–Feb (winter): rodents move indoors, cockroaches, bed bugs.
- Mar–May (summer): ants, cockroaches, flies, termites before the rains.
